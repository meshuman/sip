## What it shows

Ganesh, the son of Shiva and Parvati, is worshipped first at the start of any ceremony, trip or new venture, so that obstacles are cleared from the way. He is also the patron of learning and writing.

In the Kathmandu Valley, nearly every neighbourhood has its own Ganesh shrine, and people stop there on their way to work or before beginning something new.

## How to recognise him

- **An elephant's head** with one tusk broken.
- **A round belly**, a sign that he holds all things.
- **Usually four arms.** His hands often hold an axe, a noose, a sweet, and a broken tusk or a rosary.
- **His mount**, a mouse or rat, sits at his feet.

## Where it is placed

In many Hindu homes in Nepal, a Ganesh figure is placed at or near the main entrance, facing it, or in the household shrine. He is never placed on the floor or in a bathroom. These are customs, and families follow their own.
