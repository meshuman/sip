## What they show

In a Buddhist statue or painting, the hands carry the story. Each gesture, or mudra, points to a moment in the Buddha's life or a quality of the figure. Learning six of them lets you read most of the figures in the collection.

| Mudra | What the hands do | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Earth-touching (*bhumisparsha*) | Right hand reaches down over the right knee to touch the ground; left hand rests in the lap | The moment of awakening, with the earth called as witness. [More](/meaning/earth-touching-mudra) |
| Meditation (*dhyana*) | Both hands rest in the lap, right over left, thumbs touching | Concentration and stillness |
| Teaching (*dharmachakra*) | Both hands at the chest, thumb and forefinger of each making a circle | The first teaching, "turning the wheel of the dharma" |
| Giving (*varada*) | Right hand lowered, palm facing out | Generosity, granting what is asked |
| Fearlessness (*abhaya*) | Right hand raised, palm facing out | Protection and reassurance |
| Discussion (*vitarka*) | Hand raised, thumb and forefinger touching in a circle | Explaining and debating the teaching |

## How to recognise them

Look at the right hand first: it carries most of the meaning. Whether it points down, rests in the lap, or is raised tells you the gesture. Then look at what the left hand holds, such as a bowl, a lotus or a vajra. That often tells you which Buddha or deity it is.
