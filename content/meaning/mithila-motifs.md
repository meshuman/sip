## What they show

Mithila painting grew out of the walls of homes, painted for weddings and festivals. Its motifs carry wishes for the household: for a happy marriage, children, a long life and plenty. Painters combine them freely, so learning a few lets you read most paintings.

| Motif | Commonly read as |
|---|---|
| Fish, often in pairs | Fertility, plenty and good fortune; a pair is a couple |
| Peacock | Love and courtship |
| Bamboo | A family line that keeps growing |
| Lotus | Beauty, and the feminine |
| Sun and moon | Long life; witnesses to a marriage |
| Turtle | Steadiness and a long life |
| Tree of life | The family and its generations |

## The kohbar

The richest Mithila paintings are the *kohbar*, painted on the wall of a newly married couple's room. A kohbar gathers the lotus, bamboo, fish, turtles and the sun and moon in one dense, joyful design. Kohbar paintings on paper are a traditional wedding gift.

## Who they suit

A pair of fish or a kohbar suits a wedding or a new home. Peacocks suit a couple. A tree of life suits a family or a naming. Every Sīpa Mithila painting lists its motifs on its page.
