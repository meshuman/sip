# MakerNote

Lets an owner send thanks and thoughts to the artisan who made their piece, and shows those notes, with the maker's replies, on the maker's page.

## How it works

- **Who can write.** Only a recorded owner can write, from their owner's record or the "thank you" email sent after delivery. Each note is tied to the piece's registry number. This keeps notes genuine, which matters more than volume.
- **How the maker receives it.** Each note is translated into Nepali (or the maker's own language) and delivered the way the maker actually receives things: read aloud or printed at the workshop by Sīpa's field team, or sent to their phone. Never assume a maker reads email.
- **Replies.** The maker can reply in their own words, recorded in their language and shown with an English line beneath. A reply is optional and never chased.
- **Public or private.** The owner chooses. Notes are private by default to the maker. With the box ticked, the first name and city show on the maker's page. Never show a surname, an email or an exact address.

## Writing it

- The prompt suggests what to say ("what the piece means to you, or where it now lives") and never asks for a rating. This is a thank-you, not a review.
- Keep notes to 600 characters, with a live count.
- Confirm in place: "Sent. Raju will hear it at the workshop this week." Never use a pop-up.
- On the maker's page, show the count ("Notes from owners · 3") and the newest notes first, each with its registry number. A note is part of the piece's provenance.

## Care

Moderate notes before they are delivered or shown, and remove anything unkind or personal. Ask each maker, during onboarding, whether they want notes shown publicly. Some will prefer to keep them private.
