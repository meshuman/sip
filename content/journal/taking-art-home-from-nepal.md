Many people first see a Nepali statue or thangka in a workshop doorway in Patan or Bhaktapur. Taking one home is straightforward if you know three things.

## 1. New work can leave; old work can't

Nepal protects its heritage. Objects of historical, archaeological or artistic importance, and anything more than 100 years old, can't leave the country without government approval. New work can leave once it has been inspected and approved. If a seller tells you a piece is "antique", be wary: either it isn't, or it can't legally go home with you.

## 2. Ask for clearance before you fly

Statues and paintings are inspected before export. Ask the seller whether they will arrange it, and allow time; it is rarely same-day. {{TODO: the office, the document, the usual time}} Keep the clearance document with the piece, together with a receipt that names the maker and the year it was made. Customs at home may ask for both.

## 3. Think about weight and packing

A 30 cm cast statue can weigh 3 kg; stone weighs far more. Fragile carving and gilding need proper packing, not a sweater in your suitcase. Many travellers find it easier to have the piece cleared, packed and shipped.
