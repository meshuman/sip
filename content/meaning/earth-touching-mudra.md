## What it shows

The story goes that Siddhartha sat under the bodhi tree, resolved not to rise until he understood the nature of suffering. Mara, the lord of illusion, challenged him: who could vouch for his right to awakening? Siddhartha said nothing. He reached down and touched the earth, and the earth answered as his witness. Mara's armies fell away, and Siddhartha became the Buddha.

The gesture marks that moment: the point between seeking and awakening. It is one of the most common forms of the Buddha in Nepal, and the gesture of Akshobhya, one of the [five Buddha families](/meaning/five-buddha-families).

## How to recognise it

- **The right hand** rests over the right knee, palm inward, fingertips reaching down toward the ground.
- **The left hand** lies open in the lap, often holding an alms bowl.
- **The legs** are crossed in full lotus.

## Where it is placed

In Nepali Buddhist homes, a Buddha figure is usually placed on a shelf or altar at or above eye level, in a clean, quiet room. It is often placed facing east, the direction associated with Akshobhya, or facing the room's entrance. It is never placed on the floor or in a bathroom. These are customs, and families follow their own.
