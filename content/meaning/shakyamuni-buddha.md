## What it shows

Siddhartha Gautama was born in Lumbini, in the south of present-day Nepal, around 2,500 years ago. He was a prince of the Shakya clan. "Shakyamuni" means "the sage of the Shakyas". He left his palace life to understand suffering, and after years of practice he awakened under a bodhi tree at Bodh Gaya, in present-day India. He spent the rest of his life teaching.

A statue or painting of Shakyamuni is a reminder of that awakening, and of the possibility of it in everyone.

## How to recognise him

- **The ushnisha:** a rounded crown on the top of the head, a sign of wisdom.
- **Long earlobes:** stretched by the heavy earrings he wore as a prince and later gave up.
- **The urna:** a small mark between the eyebrows.
- **A monk's robe:** with no crown or jewellery, unlike many other Buddha figures.
- **His gesture:** most often [earth-touching](/meaning/earth-touching-mudra), meditation or teaching. [Read about the mudras](/meaning/mudras).

## Lumbini today

Lumbini is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and a place of pilgrimage. The Maya Devi Temple marks the spot of his birth, and a stone pillar raised by the Emperor Ashoka stands beside it. [Read about Lumbini](/places/lumbini).
