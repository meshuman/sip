## The place

Patan sits on a plateau south of the Bagmati river, facing Kathmandu. Its old centre is built around Patan Durbar Square, the palace square of the former Malla kings, now a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Around it lie Buddhist courtyards, called *bahal* and *bahi*, where many of the city's craft families still live and work.

## The crafts

Patan is best known for metal. Statues cast here by the lost-wax method, many fire-gilded, are found in temples and homes across the Himalaya. The city also has a long tradition of stone carving and woodcarving, visible in the temples of the palace square, including the 17th-century stone Krishna Mandir.

## Walking the workshops

Many workshops are in the lanes north of the palace square. You will hear chasing hammers before you see the doors. Some of Sīpa's makers welcome visitors; see [Visit a workshop](/visit).
