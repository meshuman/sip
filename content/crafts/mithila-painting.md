## What it is

Mithila painting is a tradition of the Mithila region, which lies across southern Nepal and northern Bihar in India. In India it is usually called Madhubani painting, after the district of Madhubani in Bihar. In Nepal its centre is Janakpur. The kingdom of Mithila was divided by the Sugauli Treaty of 1816, and Nepal kept about a quarter of it, including Janakpur.

## Where and since when

Tradition links the art to King Janak, who is said to have asked his people to paint their walls to welcome Rama to his wedding with Sita. This is a legend, not a documented history. What is documented is that women painted the mud walls and floors of their homes for weddings and festivals, and passed the skills from mother to daughter.

Writers describe five styles: *bharni*, *kachni*, *tantrik*, *godna* and *kohbar*. Kohbar paintings were traditionally made in wedding chambers. Painters today often move freely between the styles.

Painting for sale on paper began in India in the 1960s, to give women of a poor region a new income. In Nepal, the Janakpur Women's Development Centre began training women to paint for sale around 1990, and the first group there chose handmade lokta paper. Sources give 1989 or 1992 for its founding. Painting on walls is now fading in Janakpur as cement houses replace clay walls.

## How it is made

**The surface.** Early paintings were made on walls plastered with clay and cow dung. Today most work is on paper or cloth.

**The outline.** The design is drawn freehand. Traditional tools include fingers, twigs, matchsticks, nib pens and brushes made from slivers of bamboo or from rags tied to a stick.

**Colour.** Traditional colours come from natural sources: lampblack for black, turmeric for yellow, indigo for blue and red clay for red, with vermilion used as well. They were bound with gum arabic or goat's milk. Commercial colours reached the region in the 1940s, and many painters now use them or acrylics. Ask the maker which were used. **Filling.** In *bharni*, the outlined areas are filled with solid colour. In *kachni*, they are filled with fine lines and hatching. Gaps are often filled with flowers, animals, birds and geometric designs.

## Motifs

Subjects include deities and scenes from the epics, wedding scenes, the sun and the moon, plants such as the tulsi, and birds and animals. [Read about Mithila motifs](/meaning/mithila-motifs).

## What to look for

- **Hand-drawn lines** with small natural variations, not printed regularity.
- **Space filled with intent,** with patterns that follow the design.
- **A named painter** who can tell you where and how it was made. Some galleries in Nepal sell imported Indian paintings as local work, so ask.

## Caring for it

Frame a painting on paper behind glass with a mount, so that the paper does not touch the glass, and keep it out of direct sun. [Read the paper care guide](/care/paper).
