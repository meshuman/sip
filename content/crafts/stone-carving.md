## What it is

Stone carving removes stone with hammers and chisels until a design is left. In the Kathmandu Valley it has been used for temple architecture and relief, for shrines and water spouts, for small votive monuments and for figures. The carver works with the particular grain, colour and flaws of each block.

## Where and since when

Stone monuments from the Licchavi period, which ran from about the 5th to the 8th century, survive in the Valley. Among them are the small stone shrines called *caityas*, some dated to the 7th and 8th centuries, whose niches were carefully carved.

Patan's Krishna Mandir was built in the 17th century by King Siddhinarasimha Malla, entirely of stone, in a region where brick and timber were the usual materials for building.

Today, carvers' workshops stand in the streets south of Patan's Durbar Square. Much of the work is still done by hand with hammers and chisels, though some power tools are used. Skills pass down in families. The craft nearly died out in the 20th century and has revived since the 1990s, but accounts differ on how many families still practise it.

## How it is made

**Planning.** The piece is planned first, in a drawing, a clay model or a digital design.

**Choosing the stone.** Stones differ in their qualities and composition. The carver chooses a block to suit the piece and has to understand its grain, colour and flaws.

**Shaping.** Traditional chisels and hammers cut the basic form.

**Detail.** Smaller claw chisels and flat chisels add the fine detail.

**Finishing.** The surface is smoothed and polished, with sandpaper or polishing compounds.

## What to look for

- **Crisp edges** in the detail, with no crumbling.
- **No filler:** patches of resin or cement can hide a crack or a mistake.
- **A finish that suits the design:** smooth where it is meant to be, and tool marks where it is not.
- **A named stone,** because stones differ in hardness and in how they weather.

## Caring for it

Stone needs only dusting, but it can take up oil and water and stain. Keep it away from oil lamps and incense unless you accept the marks. [Read the stone care guide](/care/stone).
