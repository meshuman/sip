## What it is

The old towns of the Kathmandu Valley are known for carved wood: doors, windows, struts, pillars and *toranas* on temples, monasteries, palaces and townhouses. The carved Newar window is the distinguishing feature of traditional Newar architecture.

## Where and since when

Latticed windows probably appeared before the 12th century and became very varied under the Malla kings. The quality of carving peaked in the middle of the 18th century. Many of the techniques and designs follow medieval texts on building, the *Vastuhastra* texts such as the *Manisara*.

A famous example is the Peacock Window, *Mhaykhā Jhyā*, on the Pujari Math in Bhaktapur, where a fan-tailed peacock's feathers form the lattice. Sources date it differently, so Sīpa does not give it a date.

## Kinds of window

- ***Sanjhyā*** is a projecting bay window of three units, the classic form.
- ***Tikijhya*** is a lattice window, the most common, which lets in light and air while hiding the room from the street.
- ***Gājhyā*** is a projecting window set under a roof.
- ***Pāsukhā jhyā*** is a small window of five units, for the Five Buddhas, mostly on monastery shrine houses.

## How it is made

**The timber.** Hard sal is used outdoors because it resists weather, and it is seasoned for several years before use. Chanp, pine, cedar and sissoo are used for work that is sheltered. Sal is slower and harder to carve, and well-kept doors and windows can last for many decades.

**The parts.** A lattice window is made from three kinds of slender battens, sawn from a plank and carved. They are fitted together at right angles or at 45 degrees, and the finished lattice is dovetailed tightly into the window frame.

**The carving.** Battens carry patterns such as circles, diamonds, vajras, flowers, chains and stars. Some windows add a central figure, such as a sun, a deity or a flower. Door frames often show floral designs, gods and goddesses, the eyes of the Buddha, water pots and fish.

**The joinery.** Parts are normally assembled without metal nails or glue. Wooden pins are used in places.

## What to look for

- **Tight joints** between battens and frame, with no gaps.
- **Carving that is sharp** at the edges and clean in the hollows.
- **No filler** in joints, knots or cracks.
- **A named timber,** since sal and other woods behave differently.
