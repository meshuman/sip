## What it shows

Tara is one of the most loved figures in Himalayan Buddhism. Green Tara is the swift Tara: she acts at once to protect from fear and danger. Her green colour is associated with activity. Many people recite her mantra before travelling, or in times of difficulty.

Tibetan tradition links her to Nepal. Princess Bhrikuti, a Nepali princess who married the Tibetan king Songtsen Gampo in the 7th century, is honoured as an emanation of Green Tara.

## How to recognise her

- **She is seated:** her left leg is folded and her right leg is extended, the foot resting on a small lotus, ready to stand.
- **Her right hand** rests on her right knee, palm out, in the giving gesture.
- **Her left hand** is at her heart, holding the stem of a blue lotus that opens beside her shoulder.
- **She is crowned and jewelled.** Her body is green in paintings, and often gilded in metal.

## Where it is placed

A Tara figure is usually placed on a shelf or altar at or above eye level, in a clean room, often where she can be seen on entering. These are customs, and families follow their own.
