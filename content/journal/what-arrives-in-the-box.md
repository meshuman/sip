A piece of art usually arrives with a receipt and some bubble wrap. We think it should arrive with everything you need to understand it and look after it.

## The catalogue card

Every piece comes with a card printed on lokta, the handmade paper of the Nepali hills. The front carries the piece's name, its maker, where and over how many days it was made, and its registry number. The back says what it means, where it is customarily placed, and how to care for it. A code on the card opens the piece's record, with its film and its maker.

The card is the piece's paper certificate. Its words come from the same record as the piece's page, so the two never disagree.

## The care kit

Every material needs something different, so every kit is different.

- **Wood:** a glass flask of wood oil, the same oil its carver uses, and a soft goat-hair brush that reaches into the carving.
- **Metal and stone:** a soft brush, because they need dusting and nothing else. Polish is the worst thing you can do to gilding.
- **Thangkas and paintings on paper:** a care card, because what they need most is the right place on the right wall.

Each kit comes with a care card in English and Nepali. The steps on it are the maker's own. Refills are matched to your piece by its registry number.

## The documents

Every piece leaves Nepal cleared for export. Its clearance document and invoice travel with it, and copies wait in your owner's record in case customs at home asks to see them.

## What isn't in the box

There is no glossy leaflet about us. The piece, its maker and its record say enough.
